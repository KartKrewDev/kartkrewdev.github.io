Dr. Robotnik's Ring Racers Version 2.5 Sneak Peek

Kart Krew Dev — Tue 17 November 2026

VELOCITONI: It’s been a while! You guys seemed to like Ring Racers v2.4; while we consider the latest version the most feature complete vision, we’re gonna keep going for ourselves as extra credit to tie up our own personal loose-ends. For those not in the know, we casually drop spoilers and nuggets about both prior and future developments with no context for people to theorize about in the Kart Krew Dev Discord. We’ve let you guys think about it long enough, so we’ll confirm some theories by going into more detail now!

Some oldbies who followed closely over the years might have known this, but Online Grand Prix, aka Co-Op mode, was in the works off and on through development for a while and was supposed to be in several versions ago. Initially shelved to prioritize finishing the game, it’s back and is (or at least was!) intended to be the only headlining feature for a quick release.

But, similarly to the goal of v2.4 being a release that was intended to soften the rough edges for first-player impressions and expectations and balance the game at all levels of play (whatever the opposite of ‘death by a thousand papercuts’ was, just look at that changelog!), the current in-dev priority of “v2.5” has been to take that mentality a step further even if we already think the public facing build of the game is in a very solid state if we left it as-is and died tomorrow.

I kind of like how blunt Kart Krew is allowed to be and I do appreciate how our community has grown, learned to behave and adapt to that these days especially; it makes delivering information pretty easy. So, I’ll be honest, there are a few outstanding show-stopper bugs and missing intended features that are missing. Essentially, not only will there not be a release this year even if we’re nearing its end…but as usual with us, don’t expect anything at all in terms of dates or announcements! Ring Racers was already “done”, so we’re especially leisurely now because we’re usually prioritizing life and employment. It’s actually a healthy meme to pass around that the game is on hiatus, just so no one’s anticipating anything.

When the time eventually comes, we are considering another Release Candidate cycle to catch any final bugs and balance though like we did for v2.4!

Otherwise, for now, here is just a sliver of the changes and expectations roughly.

HEADLINERS, MAIN STAGE

Online Grand Prix

Dubbed “Co-Op” mode, this is an online version of the already existing local Grand Prix setup, which has also been touched up in a few ways, including being limited to 4-Players.

The host can set the difficulty and manually select the cup, but the voting screen for random map selection has been re-used for cup-voting as well as a good way to pass time in servers left without a manager.

Any unlocks done in Co-Op mode are reflected immediately for all players without the need to exit to the Challenge Board to activate them; although you’ll still be shown such on exit as normal. This means that friends can assist each other with their save files pretty smoothly with particular Challenge Requirements if they’re Grand Prix related, or just experience Ring Racers together blind! We’re huge fans of couch-play like games and multiplayer experiences in general, so we encourage colluding together against the CPU-players and racking up as much grading score as possible. There’s something to be said about tackling hard cups together and sharing the pain that’s far more engaging than doing it alone; the CPU count will increase with each player joined to compensate.

This mode includes getting Special Prizes, which have extra failsafe handling for the non-linear nature of them in offline mode (or so we hope, this is what testing is for…!). Behavior for these certain encounters has been re-thought out and rebalanced specifically when multiple players are available to keep it as engaging as possible and prevent ‘griefing’, something we had to address and most players probably don’t know about even though it’s possible in the current version locally.

CHECK CABLE! Even if a player disconnects in the middle of a Co-Op session, their body will pilot itself as a CPU-player for a certain amount of time until they re-join the server, keeping their seat fresh to reduce some expected frustration with this game-engine.

K-GROOVE

TYRON: Our biggest gameplay inspirations come from the arcade, a place where balancing casual friendliness and technical depth was a matter of financial life and death. The most successful games needed to hook random passerby with bright visuals and instant access to the gameplay hook, but also wanted to coax a second (third, fourth, tenth) quarter out of their wallet when it came time to “CONTINUE?”.

Sometimes that means an outright “easy mode”, like the Automatic/Manual split in many arcade racers, but fighting games have some cool variations on this; games like Capcom vs. SNK, Street Fighter Alpha 3, and even Street Fighter II Turbo’s “old” characters (see my O-Ken) tweak the existing systems to create cool styles of co-existing play. That stuff tends to stick around and create fun conversations years after release, in a way that a straightforward “Simple” control option never does.

Enter “R-Groove” and “K-Groove”, our version of Manual and Automatic Transmission. K-Groove removes most of RR’s abrupt speed changes and context-sensitive controls (1 button = 1 action), making it play more like a classic kart racer, but with the core RR “handling model” mostly intact. You probably know whether you’re in the target audience for this, but if you’re not, do me a favor and let your newbie friends use it anyway—it’s not an accessibility option or assist mode, it’s just a way to jump into the game without so much “culture shock”.

TOASTER: (Though the Tutorial does adapt in certain spots, if you want or need a fresh look. I still really love the world we built and the way it breaks down the basics, and it will always be a first-class part of Ring Racers.)

TYRON: K-Groove players can’t spend Rings on-demand for critical overtakes, and can’t use the Insta-Whip or Bail to try and reverse tough situations, but they recover quicker from damage, don’t take Ring Sting, and can pick up Rings while holding an item, which gives them a very different threat profile from R-Groove. We expect most players at the absolute top level to prefer R-Groove for its flexibility and burst speed, but K-Groove (like its namesake in Capcom vs. SNK 2) is simple, forgiving, and absolutely capable of keeping up—just don’t expect your R-Groove rivals to take it easy on you!

VELOCITONI: I think it’s also OK to confirm the naming joke about the Transmissions because it kinda sets an expectation in the menu alone; “R-Groove” is a play at Ring Racers while “K-Groove” is a play at our previous title, SRB2Kart. While it doesn’t feel like the latter exactly to a tee, veterans of SRB2Kart might find the handling-mode and lack of mental overhead very familiar regardless. I guess for our like, 13+ year anniversary we can officially pit the two vehicles against each other for fun, right?

GLOBAL MECHANIC ALTERATIONS

TYRON: I don’t even wanna put this one in its own section, so I’ll just take the bitter medicine for everyone. An “object scale” bug from SRB2Kart made turning slower than intended on most courses. We spent a long time chasing our tails on why handling felt “heavier” than before, and made a lot of worthwhile fixes doing it, but it took community mappers experimenting with extreme object scale to find the root cause—this isn’t even a Ring Racers bug, but object scale was so broken in SRB2Kart that no one was even trying to use it!

TETHERING

VELOCITONI: Our tastes have changed over time, so tethering has received a pretty major nerf. Your tether benefits are diminished the closer you are to your tether target. Previously it was just a flat out dead-zone near your opponent where it would stop functioning, but now players ease into attacking distance and can no longer pass at any time for free.

Tethering is a very load-supporting game mechanic so that probably deserved a mention, because this has massively slowed down the overall pacing of the game in multiplayer settings, down to a pace that feels a bit more familiar with our previous entry. This allows for a lot more balanced and longer moment to moment battles and cornering in tighter scenarios, everyone is moving a lot more predictably!

Kind of like K-Groove and R-Groove suggest, Ring Racers has a lot of burst movement that lets the player go at some insane speeds that aren’t usually expected in this genre and are more commonplace in anti-grav racers. This is largely a result of how Ring Racers handles friction and being “above top speed” compared to SRB2Kart, but even more so due to Boost Stacking. The biggest culprit of admittedly hard to predict speeds was always tethering, which counts as a very high priority boost to the stack.

Why?

Tethering is inspired more by Kirby Air Ride than any other game, where I wanted it to be something really strong and dynamic that mattered instead of niche impractical applications, but this also came at the cost of sling-shotting for free in most scenarios. Initially sling-shotting was something we enjoyed when we implemented it because SRB2Kart seriously struggled with catch-up mechanics. As time went though we also started mastering like hundreds of game design ways to soft-push players forward while developing Ring Racers, which creeped up how good tethering already was while we worked on the game.

By release, tether stacking was so free that it led to automatic checkmate scenarios that behind players could constantly abuse with almost no resources, leaving players actually playing the game paranoid to ever be in-front, ever. It also ended up ironically having an unintended emergent effect where it made catch-up more difficult in high-level lobbies, as the front of the pack would repeatedly slingshot off each other and hit escape velocity, so we squashed it!

Because this is such a major change, just about every other core-gameplay area was finally allowed to be touched up, all because we literally couldn’t risk it before!

DRIFT BOOSTS

MISS JARTHA: I don’t think I’ve ever written about the game like this! During basically all of 2.0 dev I was largely tuned-out of game design discussions and just focused on improving the engine and implementing game features after the details had been worked out. Just this past year though, I’ve spent a lot of time online to get a feel for how our playerbase engages with the game at a high level. Some pretty fundamental changes ensued as a result of this.

Drift boosts. This was apparently an old idea that was never realized until it got mentioned after one of our late-night netgames at like 5 am. When you perform a drift boost, there is now a “kick” that literally pushes you in the direction you’re drifting. The exact angle can be adjusted by drifting in, out, or neutral, and the strength of the kick is proportional to the color of the drift sparks, just like tri-dash. Oni and I worked really hard to get the values just right and it adds a flow to the game that feels like it always should have been there. It really made drift boosts feel important again, after ring boost kind of overshadowed that aspect of the game.

Rainbow drift boost is now especially powerful.

VELOCITONI: Drift-charge has also been looked at again; Class G now builds driftboosts at a lightning-rate. Drift-charge and the respective boost is also no longer a binary Stage 1-4 in terms of timing or power, the player now gets a smooth extra credit for getting close enough to the next stage. Charge rate is also increased based on the current gear setting, and appropriately respects Margin Boost in Duels. This keeps the intended design of patiently waiting to burst around corners while also making all classes far more balanced, we avoid binary cut-offs wherever we can!

Driftboosting in SRB2Kart was way more essential to good gameplay and gaining speed, as it was basically your only means of extra speed at most times. Now that tethering has been nerfed, we were allowed to add a more RR flavored juice to the driftboosting if you’re in R-Groove. The game is centered around driftboosts again, it’s a little nostalgic but also god damn it sure is RR-styled; “R-Eggman” is FLYING around the tighter 5 lappers.

Oh and as usual, buffs to Eggman (Class G) means that Sonic (Class C) gets indirectly buffed too. He’s like a squirrel on the bird-feeder…

TRICK PANELS

MISS JARTHA: Tyron had some ideas for Voltage and I had some for tricking itself.

There are a lot of little details that go into this and it took some back-and-forth in dev to figure out exactly what felt wrong about tricking to me. Tricking was basically being pulled in opposite design directions, between being a pause in the race and a split-second speed multiplier. It was really easy to overshoot jumps or smash into walls, but at the same time you’d spend a disproportionate amount of time hanging in the air after the trick. So we’ve slowed down the player’s velocity during the input phase and increased gravity after tricking to make it feel less floaty.

Grow and Shrink no longer affect trick speed in a weird way. Super Ring (and other bursts) no longer amplify trick speed and now simply push you forward. Super Ring specifically is also slightly weaker while tricking.

TYRON: Voltage was overwhelmingly the biggest RR offender when it came to Random Context-Sensitive Speed Changes, but it also created a lot of course design trouble; boosted speed and infinitether made it hard for mappers to pace their courses or control comeback access, which led to enormous pack gaps on courses that probably shouldn’t have had them (like 765 Stadium).

We wanted to tackle both kinds of problem at once, so after fixing the “Voltage extension” bug that plagued competition play, we split Voltage up into various colored “levels”; blue trickpanels grant you a lot of Voltage, but yellows only grant a little, and greys grant none at all.

Instead of all-or-nothing blue driftboosts, each driftboost of any color pushes you forward and removes a “level” of Voltage, so your handling is more predictable and you no longer have to specifically avoid high driftboosts. You also no longer infinitether to other players if both of you have Voltage. All that should let custom mappers use trickpanels as traversal elements a lot more freely!

ANNOYANCES

AERIAL RING RETENTION

MISS JARTHA: I think how this started was we were on Rumble Ridge one night and I drove back for the ring capsules and then promptly watched the pending ring count disappear before I even crossed the finish line.

Excess rings (above your 20 cap) collected in POSITION aren’t automatically used up until the race starts. And the same thing happens when you’re in the AIR! Now airborne ring capsules and Ringbox/Super Rings before large jumps pause their payouts until you land on the ground.

OFF-ROAD

VELOCITONI: There have been a few nerfs to the player whenever they’re in off-road, this was a long time coming. After Google itself literally spat out “SRB2Kart and Ring Racers” in response to me searching up the word “lawnmowering in kart racers” (tested on separate devices just to make sure I wasn’t insane).

We’ve been calling this ‘dirty tires’, but there’s now a dynamic lingering penalty for going into regular off-road that follows you when you leave it. If you’re in any states that would ordinarily ignore off-road then this is doesn’t apply or is removed outright from the player, like boost-based items or spindashing.

Anyone who is an SRB2Kart veteran knows our war on off-road penalties and how even community mappers struggled to reel in the exploits. This has largely been covered by Tripwire existing, but hopefully we can cover the edge cases that exist which completely invalidate several courses and frustrate custom mappers to no end. The player in Ring Racers has always had a lot of power and freedom, so there’s plenty of room to reel some things in a bit again.

This excessive power we sometimes give the player has been scope-creeped over time naturally, so expect to see nerfs in general here and there that’s geared towards a more healthy race experience that’s balanced around more parseable control and less face-tanking of all the obstacles. There’s not really a single racing game I can think of that lets the player disrespect and disrupt course boundaries as much as Ring Racers, it can be a bit embarrassing to see at times when it stops being cool stunts LOL.

INSTA-WHIP AND STUN

VELOCITONI: Insta-whip no longer causes ringloss, insta-whip no longer causes excessive stun, rejoice.

Insta-whipping went from a tool that players didn’t understand unless at high-level to -> an overcentralizing attack option with the introduction of Amps and Overdrive in v2.4. In general, a fully mobile, invincible, disjointed and unblockable hitbox MIGHT actually make it hard to balance so many aspects of the game, yeah. Expect to only see nerfs to it from here for its punishments to other players, but at least it keeps Amp rewards!

Stun has also in general been reduced, especially if you’re lower on rings!

TYRON: Anyone around me during the 2.4 RC cycle knows how much I feel like I opened Pandora’s box by programming this thing. In Race modes, I actually decreased the amount of pushback as well, so it’s less likely to send you behind walls or into pits. Get away from me.

WAVEDASHING

MISS JARTHA: People love to play the mosquito-particle-accelerator in POSITION if there is ever a sneaker panel nearby (we even did it in the 2.0 staff ghosts!) It’s pretty corny and very annoying if you get knocked around by multiple racers moving at 600% in a tight space. If you’re actively building a wavedash and bump a wall, your pinwheel size halves.

Wavedash snaking is dead (again!?) Before the pinwheel locks in, your turning direction is tracked. If you wiggle left and right it will be really hard to build your wavedash. Snaking with Invincibility and Grow is also much harder at low speeds. There’s even incentive to avoid snaking on sneaker panels – sneaker panels are a little more powerful if you drive over them in a clean arc/straight line!

EXP PROTECTION

VELOCITONI: EXP has been tweaked to be more forgiving; being in a low position earlier in the race has less immediate detrimental effects to your roulette. A lot of race starts can be down to too many variables to keep track of, especially in larger netgames, so now your mid-race play is what really counts.

But that’s not all; you now also retain your EXP value if you miss an item set at all! It’s signified by your EXP Crystal sparkling the color of your ‘missed value’ in the corner of the screen, and it’ll sparkle even harder if you miss another item set.

We didn’t have as much time as we wanted to meta-test the v2.4 balance additions, so we get to tighten up on the edge cases of those this time around; even Ringbox payouts are tightened up to encourage believable comebacks again, though our intent to not effortlessly disrupt the ability to front-run with honesty remains present. EXP still does its job, it’s just more of how we envisioned it and less of an overcorrection this time around.

TYRON: In particular, “EXP Protection” guarantees that everyone in the race will hit at least one full-power Item Box—don’t despair if you fall behind before the first checkpoint!

TRIPWIRE

VELOCITONI: When crossing through a tripwire, you gain a barrier of full invulnerability for a short duration, which gets extended the further you are from first place. This mechanic didn’t make it in time for v2.4, even though I did all the graphics for it, thank you Jartha!!!

In v2.4, Sonic Boom is a dynamic value where you can cross tripwires with raw speed the further you are behind the leader. In v2.5 internally, this value is less sudden and instead smoothly trails up and down to where it needs to go. What this means for the player is that it’s more intuitive to see it actually changing in real time, the Sub-Sonic visual is a lot less zany as to whether it will be there or not, making it easier to interact with the mechanic. Rapid changes in position that you can’t control should also screw you over significantly less and make more sense. This also reduces or even outright eliminates the viability of exploits for players waiting behind to Sonic Boom by sandbagging.

Furthermore, if you have any item that might cause a Sonic Boom on use, your Item Box will glow a Tripwire-esque pattern as an indicator with a sound queue. Whether it temporarily shows up if you’re driving fast enough while holding a Super Ring or permanently there while holding a Sneaker, we’re trying to be consistent about this. There is a lot of cheating for the sake of the player to make this work as comfortably as possible, no more guessing! Thank you Tyron!!!

MARIKO: To cap it all off with the new ability to see when your item + momentum is good to pass a tripwire, one method of seeking to pass tripwire by going nowhere has been made considerably less effective. It is much better to discourage slowing down being the right move in competitive scenarios.

WALLBONKS

TYRON: With all of our crazy speed changes and context-sensitive physics quirks, “recovery driving” can be tricky, even on the gentler courses with railings and outside walls. Wall collisions were kind of the worst of both worlds in this regard; new players often graze a wall and enter the dreaded countersteer death spiral, but strong players who can control wallbonks simply bomb into tight corners and bounce off the outer wall—often this was faster than taking the turn clean!

I went with a two-part adjustment. Wall collisions now slow you down for a little while, letting new players get their bearings and avoid pinballing around the course, but encouraging experts to take sharp corners “clean”. In return, wall collisions send you at friendlier angles; they get shorter and softer if you barely graze from the side, and actively try to push you back onto the track instead of sideways or backwards. Experts in the field will recognize this as an old arcade design technique called “lying”.

This change gets pretty important when combined with the offroad changes; players who want to keep pace with the pack will need to respect both the inside and outside of corners, balancing the shortest path with the biggest driftboost. Expect core driving to reward practice and study a lot more, especially in Time Attack!

ITEM-DRAGGING

VELOCITONI: Items no longer slow you down when dragging them behind yourself.

Tyron proposed this in a call and it was like all the air got sucked out of the room on reflex, but then we couldn’t really refute it when we thought about it. What’s that, like 3 total items or something?

SRB2Kart had this feature because the defensive play in Mario Kart was too strong and wasn’t something we wanted to replicate. Ring Racers had this penalty purely because it’s built on the same foundation; but now that we have rings as an incentive to put your shield down and just spend to go forward, it’s really antiquated. Ring Racers is actually such a hyper aggressive game that a bit of defensive stability is welcome.

TYRON: If I wanted to be really mean, I could have left slowdown enabled for K-Groove, because they play by the “old rules” in a lot of ways. But I think it’s way funnier to let everyone experience K-Bomb moving like Toguro.

ITEMS AND SET-PIECES

MISS JARTHA: California Items, we’re unforgettable…

VELOCITONI: LMAO

MISS JARTHA: So how do I even begin to talk about this? It was kind of a happy accident TBH. A long time ago, Sek made a request for a linedef/wall type that acted like a spring. And when I say long time ago, I mean 2021. So… I never did that (sorry Sek!). Later on, this year, I heard about another map Sek was making – Wacky Workbench – wherein he wanted to use sloped spring terrain on the floor but couldn’t use it because it acted exactly like a diagonal spring – completely redirecting you – and that’s not what he wanted. So I decided to do something about that and maybe make that spring linedef too!

That’s context 1. Context 2 is pretty silly: A bunch of us (not me because I was at work or something) decided to play Kart Z again, and Oni was yapping about it for days afterward. Specifically the part that was making us laugh was how all the way back in KART Z every item could just take a spring. Nowadays only Orbinauts and Jawz can do that. …At least, until now!!!

There are 3 crazy things that I did in the span of like a couple days:

  1. Wall TERRAIN exists, it only supports spring terrain right now but the potential is there for far more (imagine Dash Panels on the wall!).
  2. Every object in the game can use TERRAIN (this includes wall TERRAIN too). Items and rings get BLASTED by Dash Panels. We make exceptions for some object types, like certain VFX, but by default everything is affected by TERRAIN now, not just players.
  3. And finally, every object can use springs AND Dash Rings. And this is where it gets so crazy, because we also made it so when objects take a spring or Dash Ring, normal player gravity and normal player air drag is applied.

So now we have this crazy situation where you can throw an item at a set-piece and watch it go through like a player and come out the other side.

See you in another year when I make Jawz use Trick Panels…

ACCESSIBILITY

LAZULI: Much like its game design philosophy, Ring Racers’ visual style is heavily inspired by 90s arcade games, with bright, animated VFX and sounds accentuating the race and communicating your player state. In previous versions, the “Reduce Effects” option in Profile Setup would dampen many of these visual effects, with the most gameplay-crucial ones remaining fully intact. With help from Tyron and Toaster, however, Reduce Effects has been separated into multiple toggles to allow for more specific customization. (Special thanks to Freaky Mutant Man for his visual accessibility work in previous versions!)

TYRON: “Missing ring sounds” got bug-reported about 700 quadrillion million times.

ITEMS (SPECIFIC)

VELOCITONI: Maybe I can quick-gun some of these…

JAWZ

Jawz behavior is now more of a mix of SRB2Kart and RR in terms of its reliability as a homing item. When close to the target, it will slow down a bit to match their speed and increase its handling to stay strictly on their tail. The longer it’s in this “deadzone”, the Jawz will speed up by miniscule increments until it bops them.

This means the counterplay is more straightforwardly to:

  • Pull out your trailing item to block it.
  • Snatch the Jawz with your Insta-whip.
  • Outrun it, temporarily.

…while being much more intuitive for the actual attacker to actually aim and throw.

Oh, and if it hits you from the sides or the front it has different effects, genuinely anything to not bounce a player literally backwards. You’ll just trip over and go forwards in a stumble, but this is a rare occurrence versus every prior version of the game just because of its new steering.

TYRON: Now, instead of Jawz being nonfunctional 50% of the time and a tonsillectomy the other 50%, it has a pretty consistent threat level provided your initial shot is unobstructed. Don’t expect to trivially shake these with a wallbonk, though!

ORBINAUT

VELOCITONI: This also no longer sends you backwards most of the time if you’re hit from unfortunate angles. In turn for such a severe nerf to the item’s impact on your race, I was literally told to make it more クソゲー because it lost its charm, so I did exactly that and now you’re free to blame my friends actually.

If it hits you from the side, you’ll tumble forward but diagonally with a hard smack impact.

If it hits you from the front dead-on while you’re driving at a high enough speed, i.e. a wall rebound, then I hope you’ve played Mario Kart 64. Scarily, this is still massively preferable to what would happen to you before.

BUBBLE SHIELD

VELOCITONI: Jartha can you talk about these ones and Dapple us up?

MISS JARTHA: As much as I love to spite anyone who isn’t me and is having fun, Bubble really lost its fun factor in 2.4. Between the wall stumble penalty and 1st place fog, bouncing was basically unviable aside from really niche situations. Now the stumble has been removed and it just gives you some hitlag to “feel” the impact of hitting walls. We changed the speed curve so a fully fogged Bubble pushes you forward but is still noticeably slower than driving on the ground (instead of bringing you to a complete stop). In the back, bouncing gives you so much speed that it actually becomes a powerful catchup tool. Fog also starts further from 1st place. Plus, it’s easier to bounce up steep slopes thanks to an extra “mini-bounce” that happens after landing from the main bounce.

But the most annoying thing to me about Bubble was actually its brawling ability. It feels really unfair to be on the receiving end of an expanding hitbox that can be activated with no reaction time, sometimes with almost zero cooldown. So we added a small charge time before Bubble’s hitbox actually becomes capable of dealing damage/deflecting items.

TYRON: If the active hitbox never comes out, it also doesn’t go on cooldown, so accidental taps (or confused new players) won’t lock out the item entirely. That one always sucked to see in playtests.

HYUDORO

MISS JARTHA: Hyudoro can be thrown forward.

OK, that’s not all I have to say. One of my long-time pipe dreams was for Hyudoro to be a beast of an item. I’m talking being able to steal practically any resource. Item roulettes? Stolen. Ringbox payouts? Stolen. I’m even considering letting it suck up nearby rings like a vacuum. But these are mostly just ideas right now that I haven’t fully worked out, so you’ll just have to wait and see!

Oh yeah, I should’ve said more about forward-thrown Hyu. Its hitbox is BBL-sized and it ricochets off walls (it uses the exact same physics as Landmine). So have fun with that! Pausing to let 2nd place pass and then throw a Hyudoro at them like a lasso is the funniest thing in the world.

GARDEN TOP

MISS JARTHA: Garden Top is my favorite item. It literally uses a completely different driving model, which makes you think about levels really creatively. But you’re also incredibly vulnerable while riding a Top. Touch a map hazard, touch another player’s item, and you’re lost. I think this alone is a pretty huge barrier to players learning how to control the item. Even players who otherwise have a solid handle on the game’s mechanics usually just throw the Top out rather than take a huge risk to ride in through the level.

So we made it easier to control. Thanks to Tyron’s smart wallbonk, bumping walls with a Top usually sends you forward. At extremely high speeds, friction is increased and boosts (rings, sneaker panels) are dampened, to give you back some control. Grinding gravity has been increased in the air and fastfalling is actually fast. And the really good one: Sonic Boom aura gives you punting power, so you can just bust through hazards if you manage your speed correctly.

I really hope more players will give Top movement a chance and tap into some of the insane speed potential that it has. And if you’re worried that you’re not even going to see a Top, EXP Retention actually made it appear in the roulette way more!

FLAME SHIELD

MISS JARTHA: We’ve thought something was off about Flame Shield for a long time, even before 2.0 released. Now it’s finally been worked out! I brought it up, Tyron gave me an idea, and I found the values that worked.

Instead of the length of the meter depending on your distance from 1st place, you always start with a full meter – even 1st place themself can pick up a Flame Shield and have some fun with the item! Pressing the item button warms up the meter like before, but after you release it you’ll lose permanent meter, losing more if you’re closer to 1st place. Boost power is a lot weaker if you only heat up the meter a little bit and you’re rewarded with a ton of speed if you play it risky and hold the meter at the edge, risking burning it out.

We also fixed Flame Shield being really vulnerable if you let go of the item button for even a millisecond. Now until dash power runs out you have punting power. Flame Shield can finally bulldoze through Proximity Mines!

MEGA BARRIER (BATTLE MODE)

TYRON: I think we’ve all kind of felt [wavy hand motion] about Mega Barrier for a while, but there’s just been so much other stuff to fix—which is why I think it’s really funny that Toaster, usually our Backend Systems Warlock, arbitrarily wandered into gameplay code one day to fix it up. ROULETTE CHEESE

TYRON: I admire how straightforward this change is. With Invincigrow off the table, the rest of the game can be balanced around a less demanding (and less disruptive) level of comeback driving, which has always been the toughest part of Ring Racers for novices. (Same logic as tethering!)

NETPLAY

TYRON: Lots of small and medium fixes from this perspective. Eidolon is doing a bunch of work to make hosting and playing netgames more reliable, including IPv6 support, improved hole-punching, and (as a tandem effort) fixing multiple tricky sources of dreaded “sigfails.” Lazuli and I fixed up “Z-Votes” to display correctly during intermission and the course-vote screen, so you can more reliably run it back on Death Egg for the fifth time, and ping display is more accurate thanks to a total rewrite of the ping-measurement code.

One thing I’ve been plugging away at since pre-2.0 is the “double submit” problem, where slight fluctuations in FPS can cause a player’s networking to run “out of sync” with the server, leading to increased delay and packet loss. This was only a minor problem when playing on dedicated servers, but when playing on a “listen server” (a netgame hosted from the normal Online menu), it had the potential to be much worse, since both sides of the connection now have their own FPS fluctuations. RR now very slightly temporarily adjusts your game speed to “timeshift” you out of these situations, which can turn shaky D3 into smooth D2. The new ping calculations also help with mindelay application, which can sometimes save another tic if your natural ping was close to your Minimum Input Delay.

We don’t have rollback—that’s a project that’s well beyond our scope despite some internal attempts—but we’re still doing everything we can to make the game feel better online! Moving on from The Technical Stuff…

SEK’HEDSU: Early in dev I made a map difficulty survey that we passed out to testers to get a feel for difficulty so we could make cup orders. It… went places.

So recently I got mad about not being able to tell map difficulties at a glance and instead of going off vibes, made objective metrics for measuring map difficulty, went through every track in the game grading them on 16 different factors to come up with an actual, objective number that now shows up on voting screens so people can see how hard a map is to drive while playing it.

tl:dr: I got mad and did math and now you get another number to put in map headers. ENJOY.

TYRON: Courses now have a listed difficulty level, from 1-5 Puyos—plus the rare “BEYOND” difficulty for RR’s most punishing and abnormal layouts. Server operators can set a “maximum difficulty” if they want to cater to novice players or focus on more straightforward driving; we’ve got over 200 maps, so even the lower difficulty tiers have plenty of stuff to enjoy. We’re also planning to set up an automatic difficulty-matching system, where servers will try not to offer courses like Death Egg or Carnival Night to players who aren’t ready for them. The specifics are still being worked out, but we plan to have some configuration switches so hosts can control this behavior—but seriously, try to leave it on for at least a while, no newbie needs to drive on Thunder Piston that badly!

You may have already seen it in some other footage, but as a bow on the netplay package, we’re finally bringing back the old HOSTMOD clan feature—and just like before, players in the same clan will be placed on the same team during teamplay (if the server allows it).

ENGINE

EIDOLON: Those who’ve followed along the whole journey with the Vulkan renderer know that I am a stickler for parity with the software renderer, particularly for sprite drawing’s unusual clipping behavior. Unfortunately, I am not expecting to ever reach that goal within the constraints I set that would make the renderer a meaningful improvement over the existing OpenGL renderer. It turns out the anachronisms of the Doom software renderer are very difficult to replicate exactly in hardware while retaining all the extended functionality added to the engine over time. There might be a future for Vulkan support should OpenGL support in Windows drivers degrade further, but it is unlikely to be the overhaul I’ve tried and tried again to implement.

This has been a great learning experience at the very least; it may surprise readers to know I was working with some hardware features popularized by Doom Eternal to squeeze out the best performance possible, and the internal prototypes were showing impressive performance. Not that you really need thousands of FPS to play this game, but it’s fun to imagine it.

However, it’s not a complete loss! While the full-scale renderer rewrite is likely off the table, there’s no reason we can’t still make improvements to bring the OpenGL renderer at least closer in parity. So, OpenGL now uses the same UI rendering path as Software, which means that screen fades and scaling effects like SalCRT appear just the same as they do in Software. Various other bugs and inconsistencies are fixed as well, and further improvements continue slowly as time allows.

I’ve added some bits to ensure laptops with NVIDIA Optimus and AMD Enduro configurations use the correct dedicated GPU, which improves performance by a surprising factor on those devices (nearly half frame time/2x framerate for at least one developer). Honestly, I had just assumed we didn’t need it because drivers would detect it for us, but I haven’t owned a laptop like this in over a decade. Enjoy your free frames.

Finally, we’re migrating to 64-bit builds on Windows for future releases. Rejoice, for you can technically load every addon ever made at once—though maybe you shouldn’t do that.

COURSE-WORK

RING PANELS

SEK’HEDSU: When I came back to Ring Racers dev after the 2.4 release, and making new tracks I had some new insight on a specific issue. Watching people play, a dedicated player with vast track knowledge was pretty much able to cause a ring drought problem single handedly. Often causing other players to be very loud in calls about the lack of rings. For several tracks, we’d already started increasing ring counts across the board, but when I started making a new map I decided to use a mechanic we had had fixed, but never really took advantage of: The Award Rings linedef.

So I made Vulfax Highrise as a testing ground, a map based on one of my own personal worlds. And laid down gold panels that awarded rings over ring for driving over them for more general use, or blue panels that awarded bursts of rings and instant ring overcharge meant for shortcuts or very specific set pieces. Some testing and tweaks later (some hilarious as they didn’t have a cap when first added), and we have them in their current form. A panel on the ground that will grant you rings as you drive over it. It solved the problem of first place just denying everyone else rings, and conveniently also gave more fair trap areas as people can drop items on them that are far more visible and yet still deny the space.

They then started spreading across quite a lot of tracks in the game, replacing large clusters that caused performance problems, or giving higher incentives to go out for further ring areas by increasing the reward for breaking from tight driving lines. I’m interested in seeing how they play out once live.

CHROMATIAN: Ring Racers has always been a bit F-zero Coded, at one point hover cars were considered too! So this probably doesn’t come as much of a surprise to anyone outside of the team. It wasn’t so much of a shocker for us, either, but we did scratch our heads for a few days wondering “Why didn’t we do this so much sooner?”

Ring Panels are very similar to PIT Zones in F-Zero. Drive over one, and you will gain Rings up to a point set by mapper. Like all granular things in this game, how many you’ll get and how fast you’ll get it is pretty course dependent, but there are two general types so that you don’t have to think too hard about what’s happening: Gold and Blue Ring Panels.

Golden Ring panels fill you up to 20, the rate can vary, but it’s otherwise pretty standard stuff. Blue Ring Panels will pull you over the 20 ring cap and into overcharge. Again, how much will vary, but there’s a general range we all agree on that isn’t going to cause Black Friday at anything glowing blue.

There are 200 odd some courses here, and each set of rings on each course is placed individually. Ask a mapper in 2026 after a 3 month break to open some map with 500+ rings and adjust some turn for more and they’ll probably open Deadlock or something instead. Ring Panels provide an easy way to adjust ring counts and not think about your mouse that occasionally triple clicks as you’re placing 200 rings on a sloped turn one ring at a time.

They’re great on performance, (less Things) they’re good for visibility, (more open road without objects), and they can’t be heavily denied (they don’t respawn)....why didn’t we do these sooner? (It’s a long story…)

DIRECTIONAL TRICK PANELS

CHENGI: On the subject of changes that were made mostly to account for exploits – Trick Panels have been split between two variants – Omnidirectional and Directional.

Omnidirectional Trick Panels are the standard circular Trick Panels that work identically to how Trick Panels behave in 2.4, allowing you to steer mid-air and launch from different angles – there aren’t really many adjustments to them apart from the ones made globally. For any existing community mappers – take a breather; these are the default behavior, you won’t need to change your maps if you designed around these.

Directional Trick Panels, however – are intended to be the best friend of many a level designer who enjoys setpieces. (hi, I’m the level designer who enjoys setpieces) Unlike Omnidirectional Trick Panels, these use a rectangular design with an arrow – that specifically lock your entry angle to match the direction that the arrow faces.

Picture the first set of Trick Panels in Dark Fortress. I explicitly mention these because of how often it was easy to overshoot or approach the landing zones from weird angles. That specific section has had its Trick Panels replaced with Directional variants to encourage players to not only respect the level design a little more, but help correct any mistakes.

Players typically don’t like restrictions. In a vacuum, they’ll usually be seen as unfun. Most course elements, in the past, were designed for that kind of variability – which is not necessarily a bad thing to design around (it creates a healthy level of depth, and interesting gameplay scenarios), but we ran into a problem very frequently where sometimes we just wanted setpieces to work. So the variability reduction for these is something that will hopefully make it less stressful for both the players and level designers.

TOASTER: haha i don’t really know how to talk about my stuff… generally i will get stuck in on some big structural problem, or blast out a whole bunch of quality of life things. it’s largely unglamorous work that will make a few people really, really happy, and that’s the kind of reaction i love.

FIXES

JUGADORXEI: There’s been a major spring cleaning in our codebase to address many, many nasty bugs. On my end:

  • Wall transfers now work more consistently. In cases where players cannot initiate a wall transfer when one could start, they’ll not “latch” onto the wall anymore.
    • Wall transfers are undoubtedly awesome, so it always pained me that players would sometimes get stuck on a wall when they started one. No longer!
  • Servers will no longer be in a “softlocked” state during the end of a race if a player leaves or becomes a spectator.
    • This is a bug that has existed since the SRB2Kart days! You’ll no longer need to keep your server votes enabled to keep your server running unmanaged.
  • Texture rotation is now accounted properly for in terrain, meaning that Speed Pads (“zippers”) should always fling players into their correct directions.
    • While mappers worked around this by making the rotation of their textures the same, it clearly doesn’t work for complex texturework which requires road textures to be aligned in specific ways, so this fix ensures that we can have gameplay mechanics and art passes in levels not get in the way of each other.
    • This fix also enabled Directional Trick Panels to work, see above!
  • Dramatically reduced the amount of situations where the waypoint system could fail, count players’ progress as “cheated”, and/or give players an improper respawn prompt.
    • Long finish lines, and finish lines with solid walls surrounding them no longer cause the lap not to count, or for its credit to be removed retroactively, in various situations.
    • Crossing the finish line at odd angles, backwards or after faulting in POSITION should no longer cause lap credit to not be awarded in various situations.
    • It is now possible to cross the finish line backwards in every base game level.
    • As a result of all of these changes, various exploits have been fixed as well.
  • Multiple terrains can now be processed in a single tic, and terrain is a lot harder to “skip” over.
    • In Gear 3 contexts, such as Time Attack, going fast enough would skip over small sneaker panels, and Voltage Drop would sometimes skip over Speed Pads and other Trick Panels. Players won’t need to second-guess themselves if terrain will work in high-pressure situations now.
  • Fast Fall Bounce now happens after one tic (previously three).
    • Players would sometimes slide off the Pestilence sneaker panel platforms if their fast fall timing was off. Now what you see is what you get.
  • Fixed various checkpoint bugs.
    • If a player respawns past a checkpoint, its credit will be awarded after they touch the ground from a respawn.
    • You can no longer drive backwards to get checkpoint credit from a checkpoint you already obtained.
  • …And many more!

A lot of these have the “intern just got hired to fix a bug then leave” energy in them, because I play this game a lot, and I would experience them often, so I really felt obligated to fix these up before any upcoming release, or they would annoy me forever. More seriously, it felt important to me for the game to function properly in high speed scenarios, which is pretty common once boosting is thrown into the mix, so I wanted to dedicate my time to sort them out. Class C really did feel disproportionally affected by a bunch of them too, so I guess I am soft-buffing my main anyhow. Nonetheless, I hope everyone can enjoy playing a more stable and consistent version of Ring Racers thanks to my contributions!

MISS JARTHA: I feel the need to mention a BIG ONE. SHRINK has been optimized like nothing has in a very long time (probably not since 2.0). If you’ve experienced slowdown from Shrink being used, that problem doesn’t exist anymore. I have a pretty powerful computer, so I could put out like 20 Shrinks on City Escape before the game crawled to a dead stop. But after the optimization I can put out 100 and the framerate still exists.

TYRON: The “fixes” section of the changelog is already enormous, but cherry-picking a few of my favorites… Neutral Drift rapid-tap exploits are gone, Death Egg teleporters no longer send high-delay players to Space Cuba, drift and wavedash charge now survive vertical spring setpieces, and visual smoothness is now way better if your framerate drops below 35 for some reason.
